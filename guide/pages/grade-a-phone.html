In the grading window

  1. Run Check. Carrier, Find My and blacklist by IMEI. Wait for it: saving without it warns you. A lock found = don't buy it as a working phone.
  2. Look at the MDM line. It fills in by itself; keep grading while it says Checking MDM… (MDM and locks).
  3. Look at the battery under Device: Good (85% and up), Fair (80–84%), Service (under 80%). Under 80% or not genuine adds BATTERY SERVICE to Notes / Issues and the grade picked for you is never better than B. Your own grade always wins.
  4. Pick the Cosmetic Grade. A looks new. B normal light wear, fully working (most phones). C heavy wear, still fully working. D damaged or faulty. Each tile shows what you'd pay.
  5. Check Carrier and Color. Read off the phone when it can be. An amber Device details box = type what the phone couldn't tell us.
  6. Purchase Price. The suggestion fills in for the grade. Type over it to pay something else. No price for that phone? The window says so: type what you'll pay.
  7. Notes / Issues (optional): printed on the label exactly as the box reads.
  8. Photos (optional): Front ON, Front OFF, Back, Right, Left, Top, Port.
  9. Save & Continue. The label prints and the next phone opens (Labels).

Parts

When nothing is wrong you see one green line: Parts — Original. A replaced part shows red, like Battery — Replaced. Couldn't be checked and Not checked are grey. Parts checks have no limit: replug a phone as often as you need.

Copy a line

Click any line in the side panel (Device, Checks, Parts, Diagnostics) to copy it, for example Battery: 89%. Double-click a line to add it to Notes / Issues.

Where prices come from

Every shop starts with a full list of buy prices; prices you set yourself always win. A phone is matched to your list by its model number first, then its name. A cell that says Ask has no automatic price.
